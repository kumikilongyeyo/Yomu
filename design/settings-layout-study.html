Yomu Settings, Three Ways

Adding a source is a process, and Settings presents it as a list of preferences. Here is what is actually on the screen today, three ways to lay it out on a phone and a desktop, and which one I would build.

The actual diagnosis

These are not settings. A setting is a value you change and forget. Every row above is a step in a process with a success condition — the source answers, or it does not. A process presented as a list of preferences gives you no order to follow, no sense of progress, and no way to tell a finished step from one you never started.

The smallest honest change. Settings keeps only things that are genuinely settings; everything about acquiring sources moves behind one door that already half exists.

What it gets right

  • Five groups become four, and one of them is a door. The screen stops being a place you scroll looking for the right row.
  • State on the row. "7 answering · 1 unreachable" is the only thing you actually wanted from this screen most visits, and today you have to open /sources to learn it.
  • Nothing new to learn. Groups, rows, chevrons — the app's own furniture, rearranged.
  • Cheapest of the three by a distance. The Sources destination already exists; this mostly deletes rows from Settings and points at it.

What it gives up

  • It moves the mess rather than resolving it. /sources now has to absorb Fabric, extensions, the bridge and the manual API source, and that page gets long.
  • Still no order. A first-time user opening "Your sources" meets five ways in and no hint which to try.

What it gets right

  • It answers the question you came with. Every visit to this screen is really "why is nothing loading" or "what do I have" — and both are on the first line.
  • The states are real and already computed. Yomu tracks health per provider and demotes anything failing. Today that work is invisible; here it is the interface.
  • "Discovery only" stops being a bug report. Comick lists titles and serves no chapters, by design. A status board has a word for that; a settings list does not, which is exactly why it read as broken.
  • One add button. Extensions, Fabric, the bridge and a manual URL become four answers to one question instead of four places.

What it gives up

  • The most build of the three — a table, a side sheet, live health polling and drag ordering.
  • A board invites staring at latency numbers that do not matter. Milliseconds are diagnostic, not a leaderboard.

Paste the address of a site you read. Source Fabric will open it, work out how it is laid out, and tell you honestly whether it can be read reliably.

Already added this way — Kagane, answering.

The rail is the order; the pane is one step at a time.

What it gets right

  • It is the only one that tells you where you are. Three of five, and the two remaining are optional — nothing else here can say that.
  • Order carries the meaning. Free and instant first, paste-a-site second, your own server last, because that is genuinely the order of effort.
  • Resumable is the whole point. A tunnel is set up on a laptop on a Tuesday; the flow has to survive being left.

What it gives up

  • A wizard ages badly. It is right once and in the way for ever after, so it needs the escape hatch shown — and then you have built two interfaces.
  • Steps imply completion, and this list never completes: a new source can always appear. "3 of 5" is a comforting fiction.
  • It answers the first-run question well and the everyday question — is it working right now — not at all.
~2.5 days New flow + escape hatch Best first run

What I would actually build

A then B. A is a day and makes Settings a settings screen again: four groups, and Sources becomes one row carrying its own state. B is what that row opens, and it is the one that answers the question people bring — which of these is working — with data Yomu already computes and currently throws away.

One screen from C, not the flow. Step 5 — check they all answer — is the best idea in the study and does not need a wizard around it. It is a button on B's board. Build the rest of C only if a real first run proves confusing; on an app with one user who set it up months ago, a wizard is a screen nobody will see twice.

The thing worth fixing first, whichever shape wins

Four ways to add a source — extensions, Source Fabric, the Mihon bridge, a manual API URL — live on four pages and are described in four vocabularies. None of them says what it is for. Before any layout work: one screen, one question (what do you want to add?), four plainly-worded answers. That is most of the confusion, and it is independent of everything above.
